How It Works

Strength analysis

Everything about length, character composition, repetition, and predictable patterns is calculated in your browser. Your password never leaves the page for this part of the analysis.

Breach check

  1. Your password is hashed locally with SHA-1.
  2. Only the first 5 characters of that hash are sent to Have I Been Pwned.
  3. HIBP returns a list of matching hash suffixes — not passwords.
  4. The comparison against your full hash happens locally, in your browser.
  5. Only the result — found or not found — is shown to you.

Length — longer passwords are generally harder to guess or brute-force.

Uniqueness — reusing a password across accounts means one breach can compromise all of them.

Unpredictability — names, dates, sequences, keyboard patterns, and common phrases are the first things attackers try.

Breach exposure — a strong-looking password can still be unsafe if it has already appeared in known breach data.

Privacy

  • Password analysis happens entirely in your browser. Nothing is uploaded.
  • Your password is never stored — not in memory beyond the session, not in a database, not anywhere.
  • The breach check uses a k-anonymity approach: only a partial hash prefix is sent to Have I Been Pwned.
  • The full password is never sent to HIBP, or to any server.
  • Passwords are never written to the URL, localStorage, sessionStorage, analytics, or console logs.
  • Do not enter a password belonging to another person without their authorization.

Infernified provides an educational estimate of password strength and known breach exposure. Results are not a guarantee of security and should not be treated as a professional cybersecurity audit or legal advice.
